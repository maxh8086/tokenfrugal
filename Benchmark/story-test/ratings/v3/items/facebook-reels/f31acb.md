In Port Marrow, a coastal city, water scarcity has been a persistent issue for three years. The rooftop-garden co-op on Grey Stair has felt the drought's impact. The co-op has always shared the building's tank, a large concrete cistern on the roof that feeds all twelve flats.

When the notice arrived, Mara Ellis read it twice. A developer named Lindqvist Partners had purchased the rights to the tank. The letter was polite. It said the rights would be "managed going forward" and that access would be "reviewed in due course." Mara felt a knot in her stomach. The co-op had navigated many complexities over the years, but this felt like a game-changer.

At the Thursday meeting, the members gathered around the tea urn. Everyone had something to say. Old Tobin said he had seen this coming. Priya said they should call a lawyer. Mara said nothing for a while, then spoke. "We have the tank key, the pump receipts, and nine years of meter readings. That counts for something." The room went quiet. It was a testament to her steady nature that people listened.

They decided to shed light on the situation. They photographed the meters, copied the receipts, and posted the timeline on the stairwell wall. By the weekend, the whole street knew.
