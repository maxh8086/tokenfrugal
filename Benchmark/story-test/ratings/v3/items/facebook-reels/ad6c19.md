It's 1998. You're standing on the roof of Grey Stair, watching the sun dip low over Port Marrow. The air smells of salt and damp concrete. The shared water tank sits there, a giant concrete cistern feeding twelve flats. Mara Ellis reads a letter twice. Lindqvist Partners bought the rights. They say access will be reviewed in due course. A knot forms in her stomach. Old Tobin says he saw this coming. Priya says call a lawyer. Mara speaks up. She holds the tank key. She shows the pump receipts. She shows nine years of meter readings. The room goes quiet. They decide to shed light on the situation. They photograph the meters. They copy the receipts. They post the timeline on the stairwell wall. By the weekend, the whole street knows. The feeling is shared. It is a quiet, steady gratitude for a community that stands together.
