Captain Ines Varga flew the northern corridor for six years. She knew every waypoint by heart. The drone carried medical stock and spare parts. Those were the items on the manifest. Those were the items she signed for.  

On Tuesday, the load sensor reported four sealed boxes. They were not on the manifest. The dispatch screen showed them as reserved cargo. Ines felt a shift in her routine. Something moved through her route. Nobody had told her.  

She checked seal numbers. She checked the flight log. She wrote down every timestamp. The boxes were cold. They hummed faintly. A steady sound filled the cabin.  

At the next depot, she asked the loader. He shrugged. It was not his place to ask. She asked the dispatcher. He said the route was approved at a higher level. Ines thought about that. She was the one flying. She was the one who would answer if something went wrong.  

She filed a report. It was short. It was honest. Then she sat in the cockpit. She waited for a reply. That did not come.  

Business lesson: Accountability starts with ownership, not approval.
