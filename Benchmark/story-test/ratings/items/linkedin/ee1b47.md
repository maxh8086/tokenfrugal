[Opening Scene]
In the world of automated freight, routes are supposed to be simple. Captain Ines Varga had flown the northern corridor for six years, knowing every waypoint by heart. The drone was supposed to carry only medical stock and spare parts, the items on the manifest and the ones she signed for.

[The Incident]
On a Tuesday run, the drone's load sensor reported four sealed boxes she had never seen. They were not on the manifest; they were marked as "reserved cargo" on the dispatch screen. Ines felt a pivotal shift in her routine. Something was moving through her route, and nobody had told her.

[The Investigation]
She navigated the complexities carefully, checking the seal numbers, the flight log, and writing down every timestamp. The boxes were cold to the touch, emitting a faint, steady hum that filled the cabin. At the next depot, she asked the loader about them. He shrugged, saying it was not his place to ask. She asked the dispatcher; the route was approved at a higher level. Ines thought about that for a long time. At the end of the day, she was the one flying the aircraft, and she was the one who would answer if something went wrong.

[The Aftermath]
She filed a report, short and honest. Then she sat in the cockpit, waiting for a reply that did not come.
