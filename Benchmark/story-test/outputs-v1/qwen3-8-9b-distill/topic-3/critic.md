1. **Plot Gap: The Origin of the Caller**
   The story introduces June as a woman who calls in from the dark, but her identity, location, and motivation are never established. She is a "woman" who "gave her name only as June," yet the narrative never reveals if she is a local resident, a stranger, or a supernatural entity. The lack of any background on who she is makes her sudden appearance and disappearance feel unearned.

2. **Plot Gap: The Mechanism of Prediction**
   June claims she can describe tomorrow's weather, but the story provides no explanation for *how* she knows this. Is she a meteorologist? A psychic? A prankster? A ghost? The story treats her ability as a given fact without offering any logical or supernatural framework to explain it, leaving the central premise unexplained.

3. **Plot Gap: The Weather Service's Reaction**
   The National Weather Service issues a statement predicting clear skies, yet the story never addresses why they were wrong or whether they were aware of June's call. If a storm of this magnitude is predicted by a single random caller, it implies a massive failure in the entire scientific forecasting system, which is never acknowledged or explored.

4. **Plot Gap: The Source of the Storm**
   The storm is described as coming from the ocean, yet the town is explicitly described as "inland." The story mentions clouds rolling in from the ocean, which is geographically impossible for an inland town unless it is near a coast, contradicting the earlier description of it being an "inland town."

5. **Plot Gap: Dale's Decision to Play the Call**
   Dale decides to play the call the next morning because he "didn't want to bury it" and wanted to "see if the static would change." This decision is presented as a natural reaction, but it is not logically motivated. Why would a radio host choose to air a call that contradicts official forecasts? The story does not explain his internal reasoning or professional ethics regarding this decision.

6. **Vague Detail: The Nature of the Storm**

7. **Vague Detail: The Letter from June**
   June sends a letter with no return address and no message other than "Thank you for listening." This is a significant plot point that is left completely unresolved. The story does not explain how she sent the letter, who delivered it, or why she chose to send it at all.

8. **Vague Detail: The Ending's Resolution**
   The story concludes with Dale understanding that "the world was not a place that could be predicted, only experienced." This is a thematic statement, but it is not supported by any concrete change in Dale's behavior or understanding. He continues to host the show exactly as he did before, with no indication that his perspective has actually shifted in a meaningful way.
