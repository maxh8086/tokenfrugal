Renata Osei stared at the screen on Monday morning, the same green light blinking steadily as the Corvane Logistics fleet planner had rerouted twelve trucks away from the coast road. The output was clean, efficient—twelve trucks, forty pallets, no delays. It felt right. The planner had done its job.  

But Renata knew better. She had spent the last six years watching this system, its logic unbroken, its steps flawless. Step one: Eastmoor Depot could handle forty pallets per shift. Step two: If Eastmoor could take forty, the overflow could move to Western Yard. Step three: The Western Yard route was shorter, so the trucks should switch immediately. The chain was tight, unbroken, like a well-woven rope.  

She traced the logic back to the rulebook, the one she had reviewed a hundred times. The depot’s capacity was forty pallets per shift. That was all. No mention of docks. No mention of closures.  

Then she found it. A faded note, tucked between pages 47 and 48, written in her own handwriting six years ago: *Eastmoor north dock capacity: forty pallets. South dock closed since flood. Planner unaware.*  

Her breath caught. The planner had never seen that note. It had been buried, ignored, forgotten. The rule had been wrong. The premise was false.  

The planner had rerouted the trucks based on a lie.  

Renata typed a memo: *Eastmoor south dock closed. Capacity: forty pallets per shift, north dock only. Planner rerouting trucks to coast road.*  

She hit send. The system blinked.  

The trucks turned back.  

Twelve trucks, once rerouted to Western Yard, now headed straight for the coast road. The planner had recalculated. The route was longer, slower, but correct.  

Renata watched the screen. The trucks moved. The system worked.  

She smiled. The chain was fixed. The error was gone.  

The fleet moved. The plan was right.  

And for the first time in six years, the planner had no false premise.
