Captain Ines Varga had flown the northern corridor for six years, and she knew every waypoint by heart. The route was a well-oiled machine, with every detail accounted for. Or so she thought. As she lifted off from the depot, her drone's load sensor beeped, reporting four sealed boxes she had never seen. They were not on the manifest, and they certainly weren't on the list of items she had signed for.

Ines's eyes narrowed as she scanned the dispatch screen. The boxes were listed as "reserved cargo," but that was all the information she could get. She felt a shiver run down her spine as she realized that something was moving through her route, and nobody had told her. She had always prided herself on being a stickler for the rules, but now she was faced with a situation that made her question everything.

She navigated the complexities of the situation carefully, checking the seal numbers and the flight log. The boxes were cold to the touch, and they hummed faintly, a steady sound that seemed to fill the cabin. Ines's mind was racing with questions. What were these boxes? Who had put them on her drone? And why had they been kept secret from her?

At the next depot, she asked the loader about the mysterious boxes. He shrugged and said it was not his place to ask. Ines asked the dispatcher, but he just shook his head and said the route was approved at a higher level. Ines thought about that for a long time. At the end of the day, she was the one flying the aircraft, and she was the one who would answer if something went wrong.

She decided to file a report, keeping it short and honest. She detailed the events of the flight, including the discovery of the unknown boxes. She sent the report to her superiors, hoping for some answers. But as she sat in the cockpit, waiting for a reply that didn't come, she couldn't shake the feeling that she was in over her head.

The hours ticked by, and Ines's anxiety grew. She had always trusted her superiors to have her back, but now she wasn't so sure. She thought about the boxes, and the hum of the drone's engines seemed to take on a sinister tone. What if these boxes were more than just spare parts and medical stock? What if they were something else entirely?

As the sun began to set, Ines made a decision. She would continue to fly the route, but she would also keep a close eye on the boxes. She would monitor their temperature, their weight, and their movement. And she would be ready for anything.

The drone hummed on, carrying its mysterious cargo through the night. Ines's eyes remained fixed on the instruments, her mind racing with possibilities. She knew that she had stumbled into something much bigger than herself, and she was determined to get to the bottom of it. But as she flew on, she couldn't shake the feeling that she was being watched, that unseen eyes were trained on her and her drone. And that feeling only grew as the night wore on.
