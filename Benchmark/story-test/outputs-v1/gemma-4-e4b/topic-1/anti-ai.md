The salt-laced air of Port Marrow usually carried the scent of brine and distant fishing boats, but lately, it held a dry, dusty tang. Three years. Three relentless years of drought had turned the vibrant coastal city into a muted, thirsty expanse. On Grey Stair, the rooftop-garden co-op, a small patch of defiant green clinging to the edge of the urban sprawl, felt the pinch most acutely.

Their life, like the rest of the twelve flats stacked beneath the eaves, centered on the shared concrete cistern perched on the roof. This communal reservoir fed their thirsty tomatoes and herbs. For years, the co-op had managed this resource with practiced, if sometimes tense, camaraderie.

Then the notice arrived.

Mara Ellis found it tucked into the communal noticeboard, a crisp, official piece of paper alien against the peeling paint of the stairwell. She read it twice, then a third time, her fingers tracing the embossed logo of Lindqvist Partners. The language was maddeningly bureaucratic: the management contract for the tank had been purchased. The letter was impeccably polite, a veneer of corporate civility stretched over a threat. It stated the rights would be "managed going forward," and that access would be "reviewed in due course."

A cold, hard knot tightened in Mara’s stomach. The co-op had weathered storms—burst pipes, unexpected maintenance fees, the occasional argument over watering schedules—but this felt different. This felt like the foundation of their shared existence was being quietly undermined.

Thursday arrived, bringing the usual co-op meeting. The members gathered in the small, slightly stuffy communal room, the aroma of weak tea wafting from the battered urn. Anxiety hung in the air.

Tobin, whose memory stretched back to the days before the first drought warning, leaned heavily on his cane. "I saw this coming," he muttered, stirring his tea slowly. "These big developers, they always find a way to squeeze the little things."

Priya, ever the pragmatist, tapped her finger on the worn wooden table. "We need to call a lawyer. Immediately. Before they change the locks."

Mara listened, her gaze fixed on the condensation tracing patterns down the windowpane. The urge to panic was strong, but she felt the familiar, steadying weight of her own methodical nature settle over her. She took a slow breath, letting the silence stretch until it became heavy, expectant.

"We have the tank key," Mara finally said, her voice surprisingly level. "And we have the pump receipts. And nine years of meter readings showing our consistent usage patterns."

The room fell into a profound quiet. It was the sudden cessation of nervous chatter, the moment before a decision is forged. Mara’s quiet delivery, her refusal to escalate into hysteria, seemed to anchor the room. People looked at her, not with judgment, but with a grudging respect for her composure.

The weight of her statement hung there—a tangible counter-argument to the polished threat of Lindqvist Partners.

A long discussion followed. They debated the implications of the contract purchase, weighing the risk against the potential leverage their documentation offered. After much back and forth, they agreed: they would not react to the threat; they would illuminate the facts.

Over the next few days, the co-op transformed from a group of worried residents into a small, determined investigative unit. They meticulously photographed every meter, cross-referenced every receipt, and compiled the nine years of usage data into a clear, undeniable timeline demonstrating their established usage rights.

By the weekend, the evidence was not hidden in a dusty file cabinet. It was printed, laminated, and posted prominently on the stairwell wall, right next to the faded notice from Lindqvist Partners. The timeline, stark and factual, laid bare the history of their shared stewardship.

The news spread through Grey Stair like wildfire. By Monday morning, the entire street knew. The polite, bureaucratic threat had been met not with a plea, but with a meticulously documented history, and in the thirsty, unforgiving expanse of Port Marrow, facts proved a powerful, life-giving resource.
