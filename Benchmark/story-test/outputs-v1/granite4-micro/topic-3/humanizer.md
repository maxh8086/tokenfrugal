In the small inland town of Harrow Creek, WKRV's midnight show was the only voice many people trusted after dark. Dale Ransome, the host, had run the show for eleven years. He was known for taking every call seriously, even the strange ones.

One such call came in at 12:14. The caller, a woman who gave her name only as June, said she could describe tomorrow's weather. Dale was skeptical, but he let her talk. She said the rain would start at the water tower around six in the morning, that the wind would turn east by noon, and that the hail would come just after three. She sounded calm, almost bored, as if she were reading a shopping list.

Dale felt a shift in the room. He checked the forecast sheet on his desk. It said clear skies. He thought about that for a while. The studio hummed. At the end of the day, he decided to play the call the next morning.

The next day, the rain began at 6:02 at the water tower. The wind turned east by eleven. The hail came at 3:10. Dale sat in his car in the station lot and watched the sky turn grey. He had no explanation, and he did not pretend to have one.

June never called back. The station received a few emails, a flood of questions, and one letter with no return address. Dale kept the letter in his drawer, next to the old forecast sheet, and he never again said the word "clear" on air.
