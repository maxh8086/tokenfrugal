In the small inland town of Harrow Creek, the late-night radio show on WKRV was a beacon of light for many residents. It was the only voice they trusted after the sun had set, and the host, Dale Ransome, had been at the helm for eleven years. Dale was known for taking every call seriously, no matter how strange they might be.

One such call came in at precisely 12:14. The caller, a woman who only identified herself as June, claimed she could predict the weather for the following day. Dale, naturally, was skeptical, but he allowed her to speak. She said the rain would begin at the water tower around six in the morning, that the wind would shift to the east by noon, and that the hail would arrive just after three. She spoke with a calmness, almost as if she were reading a shopping list.

There was an undeniable shift in the room as June spoke. Dale checked the forecast sheet on his desk. It read clear skies. He mulled over this for a moment. The studio hummed with anticipation. At the end of the day, he decided to play the call the following morning.

The next day, the rain began at 6:02 at the water tower. The wind shifted to the east by eleven. The hail arrived at 3:10. Dale sat in his car in the station lot, watching the sky turn grey. He had no explanation, and he did not pretend to have one.

June never called back. The station received a few emails, a flood of questions, and one letter with no return address. Dale kept the letter in his drawer, next to the old forecast sheet, and he never again said the word "clear" on air.
