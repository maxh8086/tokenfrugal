**The Unmarked Cargo**

The drone’s ascent cut through the northern sky like a blade through still air. Six years of this route—six years of medical supplies, of spare parts, of the quiet weight of duty pressing against her ribs. But today, the load sensor glitched. Four boxes. Four unlisted boxes. Their presence was a lie tucked into the drone’s belly, silent as frost on a window.

Ines traced the seal numbers with her thumb. Official. Tampered only by the depot’s cold, mechanical fingers. She cross-referenced timestamps, watched the flight log scroll like a confession. The boxes had slipped in without a trace. No origin. No destination. Just a hum—low, rhythmic, thrumming through the drone’s frame like a heartbeat buried beneath the metal.

The loader at the next stop exhaled smoke through his lips, shoulders hunched against the cold. *"Not my concern,"* he said, already turning away. The dispatcher’s gaze didn’t lift from her screen. *"Higher clearance,"* she murmured. *"You’re good to go."*

Ines stood in the loading bay, the weight of the unknown pressing against her chest. She was the one who’d fly. She was the one who’d answer if something broke.

That night, she typed her report. No fluff. No excuses. The boxes. Their seals. The cold. The hum that seeped into the drone’s bones. She hit send.

No reply.

---

The tundra stretched below her, a wound in the earth, the wind howling like a warning. The boxes remained. Silent. Watching. Ines clenched the controls, her mind racing through possibilities. Who had added them? Why? What were they carrying?

She remembered the last time she’d questioned something—the missing vaccines, the "misaligned" cargo. Both times, the answer had been the same: *"Operational error."* But this wasn’t error. This was something else.

By the third day, the hum wasn’t just in the boxes anymore. It was in the drone’s frame, a vibration that made the instruments flicker like a dying light. Ines checked the diagnostics. Nothing. *Too* stable. Like a machine pretending to be alive.

She landed at the next depot. The loader was younger now, his hands shaking as he avoided her eyes. *"Just… keep flying,"* he whispered.

Ines didn’t.

She pulled up the manifest again. No record of the boxes being removed. No notes. No transfers. Just them. Waiting.

She wrote another report. Longer this time. The hum. The diagnostics. The way the boxes seemed to *listen* to the drone’s systems. She sent it.

Still nothing.

---

The final leg stretched on, the air thinning like a held breath. The boxes hummed now—a steady, insistent pulse, like a machine waking from hibernation. Ines’s fingers trembled on the controls. She had never felt so alone.

She thought of the last time she’d been told to stop digging. The time she’d been warned to let things go. She didn’t listen then. She wouldn’t now.

The depot was empty when she landed. Only the boxes remained, their hum filling the silence. Ines ran her gloved hand over their surface. Cold. Smooth. No labels. No warnings.

Then she saw it—a mark, barely there, pressed into the metal near the seal. A triangle. A circle. A line. She had seen it before. In the reports of the missing cargo. In the way depot workers shifted when she asked questions. In the eyes of those who knew too much.

She pulled out her terminal and dialed. The line rang once. Twice.

A voice, rough with urgency: *"Ines. You’re not supposed to be here."*

She didn’t answer. She just sent the final report.

And then she flew.
