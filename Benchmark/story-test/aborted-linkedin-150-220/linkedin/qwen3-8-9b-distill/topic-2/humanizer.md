You sign off on a manifest. You trust the system. You expect the cargo to match the paperwork.

Captain Ines Varga flew the northern corridor for six years. She knew every waypoint by heart.

On Tuesday, her drone load sensor reported four sealed boxes. She had never seen them. They were not on the manifest. The dispatch screen labeled them "reserved cargo."

Ines felt a shift in her routine. Something was moving through her route. Nobody had told her.

She checked the seal numbers. She checked the flight log. She wrote down every timestamp. The boxes were cold to the touch. They hummed faintly. The sound filled the cabin.

At the next depot, she asked the loader. He shrugged. He said it was not his place to ask. She asked the dispatcher. The dispatcher said the route was approved at a higher level.

Ines thought about that for a long time. She was the one flying the aircraft. She was the one who would answer if something went wrong.

She filed a report. It was short. It was honest. Then she sat in the cockpit and waited for a reply that did not come.

The lesson is clear. When your data does not match your reality, you must act.

What would you have done if you saw unlisted cargo on your manifest?
