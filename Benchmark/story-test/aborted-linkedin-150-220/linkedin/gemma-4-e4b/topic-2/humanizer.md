The automated freight route seemed routine. Captain Ines Varga flew the northern corridor for six years. Her drone carried only medical stock. She knew every waypoint perfectly.

One Tuesday, the load sensor flagged four unknown boxes. They were not on the manifest. The system labeled them "reserved cargo." Ines felt a subtle shift. Something moved on her route.

She investigated methodically. She checked seal numbers and flight logs. The boxes felt cold. They hummed faintly. At the next depot, the loader was unhelpful. The dispatcher cited approvals.

Ines reflected on this. She was the pilot. She held flight responsibility. She filed a concise report. Then, she waited for a response.

Integrity demands transparency. Procedure alone is not enough. When processes hide reality, risk grows.

How do you balance strict protocol with the need to question unseen operational changes?
