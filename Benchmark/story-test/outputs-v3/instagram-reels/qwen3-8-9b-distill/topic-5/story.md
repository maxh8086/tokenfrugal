Have you ever trusted a "clearly" that turned out to be wrong?

Retired mathematician Helena Voss scribbled a proof on an envelope.

She believed it was a tapestry of old ideas woven into something new.

Her former student, Tomas, called it beautiful.

The argument ran for three pages.

It rested on one small lemma about intersecting surfaces.

Helena wrote "clearly" above it and moved on.

Nobody had proved it.

Tomas read the envelope on a train.

He tested small cases on the margin of his notebook.

The lemma held for every example he tried.

But a pattern is not a proof.

They spent the next month racing to close the gap.

Helena wrote long messages at night.

Tomas replied in the early morning with sketches and counterexamples.

On the final Friday, Tomas found a route through a covering argument.

It was not the proof Helena had imagined, but it worked.

They sat together in her kitchen and checked it twice.

The lemma was no longer a gap.

It was a step.
