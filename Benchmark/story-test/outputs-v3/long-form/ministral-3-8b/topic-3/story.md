**Dale Ransome adjusted the headphones, the studio’s fluorescent glow casting long shadows across his desk.** The clock read 12:14—midnight, and the phone line hummed like a live wire. He exhaled, fingers hovering over the receiver. Eleven years of late-night forecasts had taught him to listen, even to the impossible.

The voice on the other end was steady, almost indifferent. *"June,"* she said, no more. *"Rain starts at the water tower at six. Wind turns east by noon. Hail at three."* The words hung in the silence like a dare. Dale’s pen paused mid-air. The forecast sheet on his desk read *clear skies*. His breath caught. The studio’s clock ticked, slow and deliberate.

He made a decision. *"June,"* he said, voice low. *"I’ll play your call tomorrow."* The line went dead.

---

The next morning, Dale sat in his car, engine off, as the sky darkened earlier than expected. At 6:02, the first drops hit the windshield. By 11:00, the wind howled from the east. At 3:10, hail pelted the station roof like angry pebbles. He watched, numb, as the forecast sheet crumpled in his hands.

No more calls. No explanations. Only the letter—no address, just a single sentence: *"Some things aren’t meant to be understood."* Dale folded it carefully, placing it beside the ruined paper. From then on, he never said *clear* again. The air between them had shifted. Some truths were better left unspoken.
