Dale Ransome sat in the dimly lit studio of WKRV, the only sound the soft hum of equipment at 12:14 AM.

A woman's voice came on the line, calm and detached. "Hello, I'm June. I can describe tomorrow's weather." Dale's skepticism was piqued, but he let her continue.

She rattled off a list of precise details: rain at the water tower at 6 AM, wind turning east by noon, hail just after 3 PM. Dale's eyes narrowed as he checked the forecast sheet on his desk. Clear skies.

He thought about it for a moment, the studio's silence a stark contrast to June's matter-of-fact tone. He decided to play the call the next morning.

The next day, Dale sat in his car in the station lot, watching the sky turn grey. The rain began at 6:02 at the water tower, the wind turned east by 11, and the hail came at 3:10. He had no explanation.

Dale's eyes drifted to the letter in his drawer, the one with no return address. He had kept it there, next to the old forecast sheet, a reminder of the strange call. He never again said the word "clear" on air.
