In the quiet town of Harrow Creek, late-night radio host Dale Ransome, WKRV's midnight show, was the only trusted voice after dark. Dale, with eleven years of experience, took every call seriously, even the peculiar ones. At 12:14, a woman named June called, claiming she could predict tomorrow's weather. Dale was skeptical but allowed her to speak. She predicted rain at the water tower at six, wind turning east by noon, and hail at three. Her calm demeanor suggested she was reading a list. A shift in the room made Dale check the forecast sheet, which said clear skies. He pondered this for a moment. The studio hummed. The next morning, Dale decided to play the call. As predicted, the rain began at six, the wind turned east by eleven, and the hail came at three. Dale watched the sky turn grey in his car. No explanation was found, and he didn't pretend to have one. June never called back. The station received emails and questions, and one letter with no return address. Dale kept the letter in his drawer next to the old forecast sheet and never again said "clear" on air.
