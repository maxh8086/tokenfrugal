Captain Ines Varga flew the northern corridor for six years. She knew every waypoint by heart. The drone carried only medical stock and spare parts. Those were the items on the manifest. She signed for them personally.

On Tuesday, the load sensor reported four sealed boxes. She had never seen them before. They were not on the manifest at all. The dispatch screen showed them as reserved cargo. Ines felt a pivotal shift in her routine. Something was moving through her route without notice.

She navigated the complexities of the situation carefully. She checked the seal numbers. She checked the flight log. She wrote down every timestamp. The boxes were cold to the touch. They hummed faintly inside the cabin.

At the next depot, she asked the loader about them. He shrugged and said it was not his place to ask. She asked the dispatcher. The dispatcher said the route was approved at a higher level. Ines thought about that for a long time. At the end of the day, she was the one flying the aircraft. She was the one who would answer if something went wrong.

She filed a report. It was short and honest. Then she sat in the cockpit and waited for a reply that did not come.

What would you do if your supervisor ordered you to ignore a safety violation?
