Captain Ines Varga flew the northern freight corridor for six years. She knew every waypoint by heart and trusted her manifest completely. One Tuesday, her drone's sensor reported four sealed boxes she had never seen. These items appeared on the screen as reserved cargo, yet they were not on the official list. Ines felt a pivotal shift in her routine as something moved through her route without authorization. She navigated the complexities carefully, checking seal numbers and flight logs meticulously. The boxes hummed faintly, a steady sound that filled the quiet cabin. At the next depot, the loader shrugged and said it was not his place to ask. The dispatcher claimed the route was approved at a higher level. Ines realized she was the one flying the aircraft and answering for any errors. She filed a short, honest report and waited for a reply that never came. The lesson is that operational integrity requires you to verify every load before accepting responsibility. What specific verification step do you perform before accepting a new shipment?
