In the world of automated freight, simplicity is key. Captain Ines Varga, with six years of experience on the northern corridor, knew every waypoint by heart. The drone was supposed to carry only medical stock and spare parts, items on the manifest and signed for. On Tuesday, the drone's load sensor reported four sealed boxes not on the manifest. They were "reserved cargo." Ines felt a shift in her routine. She carefully checked the seal numbers, flight log, and timestamps. The boxes were cold and hummed faintly. At the next depot, the loader shrugged, and the dispatcher confirmed the route was approved at a higher level. Ines, the one flying the aircraft and answering for any mishap, filed a report. It was short and honest. The reply never came. This situation teaches us the importance of understanding our role and responsibilities in the supply chain.
