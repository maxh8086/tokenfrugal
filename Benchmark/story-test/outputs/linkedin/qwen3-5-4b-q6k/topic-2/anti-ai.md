In automated freight, routes are simple. Captain Ines Varga flew the northern corridor for six years. She knew every waypoint by heart. Her drone carried only medical stock and spare parts. Those items were on the manifest. She signed for them personally.

On Tuesday, the load sensor reported four sealed boxes. They were never on the manifest. The dispatch screen labeled them "reserved cargo." Ines felt a shift in her routine. Something moved through her route without notice.

She checked seal numbers and flight logs. She recorded every timestamp. The boxes were cold to the touch. They hummed faintly inside the cabin.

At the next depot, she asked the loader. He shrugged and said it was not his place. She asked the dispatcher. He claimed the route was approved at a higher level. Ines thought about that for a long time. She was the one flying the aircraft. She would answer if something went wrong.

She filed a short, honest report. Then she sat in the cockpit. She waited for a reply that never came. Authority cannot override operational responsibility. Verify data before accepting instructions blindly. Trust your training over unverified claims.
