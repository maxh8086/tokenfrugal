The late-night air in Harrow Creek hummed with static. WKRV's midnight show was the only voice people trusted after dark. Host Dale Ransome had run the program for eleven years. He took every call seriously, even the strange ones.

A call came in at 12:14. A woman named June described tomorrow's weather with eerie precision. She said rain would start at the water tower at six. The wind would turn east by noon. Hail would fall just after three. She sounded calm, almost bored.

Dale felt a shift in the room. He checked his forecast sheet. It said clear skies. He thought about that for a long time. The studio hummed softly around him. At the end of the day, he decided to play the call the next morning.

The next day, the rain began at 6:02 at the water tower. The wind turned east by eleven. Hail came at 3:10. Dale sat in his car in the station lot. He watched the sky turn grey. He had no explanation. He did not pretend to have one.

June never called back. The station received a flood of questions. One letter arrived with no return address. Dale kept the letter in his drawer. He placed it next to the old forecast sheet. He never again said the word "clear" on air.
