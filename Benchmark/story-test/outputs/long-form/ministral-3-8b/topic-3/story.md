**FORECAST CALLER**
*Three-Act Script*

---

### **ACT I: SETUP**
*(The air in Harrow Creek is thick with the scent of damp pine and old oil lamps. The town hums like a clockwork beast, its gears grinding through the long night. WKRV’s radio studio is a relic—wallpaper peeling, a flickering neon sign spelling out "LIVE" in cracked letters. Dale Ransome, gaunt and perpetually dusted with static, adjusts his headphones. The clock ticks toward midnight.)*

**DALE** *(into mic, weary)*
"Harrow Creek, this is Dale. If the sky’s a liar tonight, you’re not alone."

*(A static crack. Then—)*

**JUNE** *(voice smooth as river ice)*
"Six AM. Water tower. Rain starts there."

*(Dale blinks. His fingers hover over the forecast sheet—"Clear. 72°." He exhales through his nose.)*

**DALE** *(skeptical)*
"Ma’am, the weatherman says—"

**JUNE**
"Wind turns east by noon. Hail at three sharp."

*(The line goes dead. Dale stares at the phone. The studio’s old fan wheezes. Outside, a coyote howls. He scribbles the details on a napkin.)*

---

### **ACT II: CONFLICT**
*(Dawn creeps in through grimy windows. Dale’s car sits in the station lot, engine off. The sky is still blue. But at 6:02 AM, the water tower’s shadow stretches long—then *spits*. A single drop hits the windshield. Then another. By 6:05, it’s a storm. Dale’s breath quickens. His hands grip the wheel.)*

**DALE** *(muttering)*
"Goddamn—"

*(By noon, the wind *shifts*. Leaves skitter like rats. At 3:10 PM, the sky *teethes*. Hail the size of quarters smashes the car’s roof. Dale doesn’t move. He just watches, numb. The radio crackles—someone’s voice, panicked—but he doesn’t answer. The storm doesn’t care about calls.)*

*(Later, in the station, he opens the letter. No stamp. No name. Just one sentence, scrawled in ink that smells like wet earth: "You’re not the first to listen.")*

---

### **ACT III: RESOLUTION**
*(Dale stands in the empty studio. The forecast sheet lies beside the letter. He picks up the phone. The line is dead. He hangs up. Then, slowly, he reaches for the mic.)*

**DALE** *(soft, final)*
"Tomorrow? Tomorrow, we’ll see."

*(He doesn’t say "clear" again. The town holds its breath. Somewhere, a radio turns on. The storm’s echoes fade into silence. But the air hums—just once—like a tuning fork left too long in the dark.)*
