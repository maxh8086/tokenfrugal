Ines Varga had been a captain in the automated freight service for six years, and she knew the northern corridor like the back of her hand. The route was simple, with waypoints she could recite in her sleep. Her drone was supposed to carry only medical supplies and spare parts, items meticulously listed on the manifest. But on the Tuesday run, something felt off.

The drone's load sensor reported four sealed boxes that Ines had never seen before. They were not on the manifest. The dispatch screen showed them as "reserved cargo." Ines felt a shift in her routine. Something was moving through her route, and nobody had told her.

She checked the seal numbers, a series of alphanumeric codes that seemed to be the only identifiers for the boxes. She checked the flight log, hoping to find some clue, but the entries were blank. Ines wrote down every timestamp, a meticulous record-keeper in the face of uncertainty.

The boxes were cold to the touch, and they hummed faintly, a steady sound that seemed to fill the cabin. Ines couldn't shake the feeling that something was amiss. She knew the route like the back of her hand, but these boxes were a mystery.

At the next depot, Ines asked the loader about the boxes. He shrugged and said it was not his place to ask. She asked the dispatcher. The dispatcher said the route was approved at a higher level. Ines thought about that for a long time. She was the one flying the aircraft, and she was the one who would answer if something went wrong.

Ines filed a report. It was short, and it was honest. She wrote down the details of the boxes, their temperature, and the hum they emitted. She noted the timestamps and the fact that they were not on the manifest. Then she sat in the cockpit and waited for a reply that did not come.

Days passed, and Ines continued her route, her mind consumed by the boxes. She checked the boxes every time she landed, but they remained sealed and silent. The hum was constant, a reminder of the mystery that hung over her.

Ines's routine became a strange mix of the mundane and the mysterious. She would land at each depot, check the boxes, and then continue on her way. The hum of the boxes grew louder, a constant reminder of the unknown cargo. Ines began to wonder if the boxes were a test, a challenge to her integrity and her duty as a captain.

One night, as Ines was flying over the northern corridor, the hum of the boxes reached a crescendo. She felt a strange sensation, a mix of fear and curiosity. She decided to land at the next depot, a small outpost in the middle of nowhere.

Ines landed the drone and approached the boxes. She opened the first one, and inside was a small, intricately carved wooden box. She opened it, and inside was a single, delicate seed. The seed was unlike anything she had ever seen, a deep, almost iridescent green. She closed the box and placed it back, her mind racing with questions.

Ines decided to take the seed with her. She placed it in a small, insulated container in the drone's cargo hold. She continued her route, the seed a constant companion, a reminder of the mystery that had been thrust upon her.
