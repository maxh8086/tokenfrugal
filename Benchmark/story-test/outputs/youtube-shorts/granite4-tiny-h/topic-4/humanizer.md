[Opening shot: A fleet planner at Corvane Logistics, typing on a computer]

Narrator: The fleet planner at Corvane Logistics was built to reason through complex routing problems, and it did so in a clear, step-by-step way.

[Cut to: The planner's screen displaying the routing process]

Narrator: Its reasoning was only as good as the rules it inherited. Step one read: Eastmoor's capacity was forty pallets per shift, but only on the north dock.

[Cut to: The planner adjusting the routing, moving twelve trucks away from the coast road]

Narrator: Step two followed: If Eastmoor could take forty pallets, then the overflow could move to Western Yard. Step three followed: The Western Yard route was shorter, so the fleet should switch immediately.

[Cut to: The planner reviewing the output]

Narrator: Analyst Renata Osei read the output on Monday morning. Something felt off.

[Cut to: Renata navigating through the old rule book]

Narrator: She found the original note from six years earlier. Eastmoor's capacity was forty pallets per shift, yes, but only on the north dock. The south dock had been closed since a flood.

[Cut to: Renata shedding light on the error in a short memo]

Narrator: The planner had no way to know that. Its conclusion was not wrong in its logic. It was wrong in its first premise, and every later step inherited that mistake.

[Cut to: The planner realizing the mistake]

Narrator: At the end of the day, the chain of reasoning had been pivotal to the decision, and it had been built on a single false line in a document nobody reread.

[Cut to: Renata updating the rule, rerunning the planner]

Narrator: Renata updated the rule, reran the planner, and watched the trucks turn back toward the coast road.
