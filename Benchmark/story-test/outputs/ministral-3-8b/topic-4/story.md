**"The Weight of the Old Rules"**

The morning sun slanted through the grimy windows of Corvane Logistics’ planning office, casting long shadows over the blueprints and half-empty coffee cups. Renata Osei leaned back in her chair, rubbing her temples. The fleet planner’s output had been clean, efficient—too clean. It had rerouted twelve trucks away from the coast road with the precision of a well-oiled machine, but something gnawed at her.

She traced the logic step by step, as if following a path she’d walked a hundred times before.

*Step one: Eastmoor depot has a loading capacity of forty pallets per shift.*

Renata frowned. She knew that number. It was the first rule in the old rulebook, etched in by some long-forgotten planner years ago. But she also knew—*felt*—that it wasn’t the whole story.

She flipped through the yellowed pages of the manual, past updates and marginalia, until she found it: a faded note scrawled in ink, dated six years prior. *North dock only. South dock closed after flood. Capacity remains forty, but only north side.*

The planner had never seen that note.

Renata exhaled sharply. The logic hadn’t failed. It had been *blind*.

She spent the next hour tracking down the truth. The south dock had been sealed off after the storm, its rusted gates welded shut. The rulebook had been updated—somewhere—but not in the version the planner used. And no one had thought to check.

By noon, she’d drafted a memo, short and sharp:

*"The fleet planner’s rerouting decision is based on an outdated assumption. Eastmoor’s capacity is forty pallets, but only on the north dock. The south dock remains closed. The Western Yard route is invalid. Correcting this will require immediate adjustment."*

She hit send.

The office phone buzzed. It was the night shift supervisor.

*"Renata? The trucks are already turning back toward the coast road."*

She didn’t need to ask why. The planner had corrected itself the moment the rule was fixed. The chain had been broken—and rebuilt.

Later, as she packed up her things, she glanced back at the rulebook. Somewhere in its pages, a single line had carried the weight of twelve trucks, a dozen wasted hours, and a decision that could have cost more than just time.

She closed the book.

The next update would be clearer.
