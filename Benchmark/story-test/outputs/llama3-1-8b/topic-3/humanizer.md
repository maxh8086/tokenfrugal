The midnight show on WKRV was a staple of Harrow Creek's late-night airwaves. For eleven years, Dale Ransome had been the voice of the town after dark, and he took every call seriously, no matter how strange. The studio was a dimly lit room, filled with the soft hum of equipment and the occasional creak of the old building. Dale sat at his desk, a cup of cold coffee in front of him, as he waited for the next caller.

The phone rang at 12:14, and Dale's eyes flicked to the clock on the wall. He picked up the receiver, his voice smooth as he said, "Welcome to WKRV's midnight show. I'm your host, Dale Ransome. What's on your mind tonight?"

The caller, a woman who introduced herself only as June, spoke in a calm, almost bored tone. "Hi, Dale. I wanted to talk to you about the weather tomorrow."

Dale's interest piqued, he leaned forward in his chair. "Go on."

June continued, "I can tell you exactly what the weather will be like tomorrow. The rain will start at the water tower around six in the morning, the wind will turn east by noon, and the hail will come just after three."

Dale's eyes scanned the forecast sheet on his desk, his mind racing with the implications. The sheet said clear skies, but he couldn't shake the feeling that June was onto something. He decided to play it cool, to see where this was going. "That's... quite a prediction, June. Can you tell me how you know this?"

June's response was matter-of-fact. "I just do. I've been watching the weather patterns for a while now, and I'm pretty sure I've got it right."

Dale felt a shiver run down his spine as he hung up the phone. He sat in silence for a moment, his mind racing with the possibilities. He checked the forecast sheet again, but it still said clear skies. He thought about calling the weather service, but something held him back.

The next day, Dale sat in his car in the station lot, watching the sky turn grey. The rain began at 6:02 at the water tower, just as June had predicted. The wind turned east by eleven, and the hail came at 3:10. Dale felt a sense of wonder, mixed with a healthy dose of skepticism. He had no explanation for June's prediction, and he didn't pretend to have one.

The station received a flood of calls and emails, all asking about June's prediction. Dale tried to downplay it, to say that it was just a weird coincidence, but he couldn't shake the feeling that something strange had happened. He kept the letter that came in the next day, with no return address, in his drawer, next to the old forecast sheet. It was a simple note, with only a few words: "You should have believed her."

Dale never again said the word "clear" on air. He couldn't explain June's prediction, but he knew that he had been a part of something strange and mysterious. The midnight show on WKRV continued, but it was never the same. Dale's voice was a little softer, a little more cautious, as he waited for the next caller to come on the air.
