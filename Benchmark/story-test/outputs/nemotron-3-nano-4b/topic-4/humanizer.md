Renata stared at the screen. The green light blinked. Twelve trucks. Forty pallets. No delays.  

She had watched this system for six years. The logic was flawless. Step one: Eastmoor Depot handled forty pallets per shift. Step two: Overflow moved to Western Yard. Step three: The route was shorter. The chain was tight.  

She traced the rulebook. Forty pallets per shift. That was all. No docks. No closures.  

Then she found it. A note. Her handwriting. Six years ago.  

*Eastmoor north dock capacity: forty pallets. South dock closed since flood. Planner unaware.*  

Her breath caught. The planner had never seen it. It had been buried. Ignored. Forgotten.  

The rule was wrong. The premise was false.  

She typed a memo.  

*Eastmoor south dock closed. Capacity: forty pallets per shift. North dock only. Planner rerouting trucks to coast road.*  

She hit send.  

The system blinked.  

The trucks turned back.  

Twelve trucks. Once rerouted to Western Yard. Now headed straight for the coast road.  

The planner recalculated. The route was longer. Slower. But correct.  

Renata watched. The trucks moved. The system worked.  

She smiled. The chain was fixed. The error was gone.  

The fleet moved. The plan was right.  

And for the first time in six years. The planner had no false premise.
