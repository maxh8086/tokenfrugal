The northern corridor was Ines Varga’s world. Six years of silent flights, predictable waypoints, and a drone that carried only what the manifest said: medical stock and spare parts. She knew every turn, every altitude, every heartbeat of the route. The drone was her compass, her companion, her responsibility. She signed for the cargo, not the mystery.  

Tuesday came with a shift. The load sensor blinked—four sealed boxes, cold and humming, their presence unlisted on the manifest. “Reserved cargo,” the dispatch screen read. Ines froze. Her routine, her rhythm, was broken. Something was moving through her path, and no one had told her.  

She didn’t panic. Not yet. She checked the seal numbers—each one unique, each one stamped with a code she’d never seen. She cross-referenced the flight log, timestamp by timestamp, noting the exact moment the drone entered the northern corridor and the moment the boxes appeared. The hum was steady, a low thrum that vibrated through the cabin, like a heartbeat beneath the silence. The boxes were cold, not from the air, but from something deeper, something that had been waiting.  

At the next depot, she asked the loader. He shrugged. “Not my place to ask.” She asked the dispatcher. He said the route was approved at a higher level. Ines sat in the cockpit, the hum still in her ears, and thought: *I am the one flying. I am the one who answers.* The drone was her eyes, her voice, her final word. If something went wrong, she would be the one to explain it.  

She filed the report. Short. Honest. No speculation. Just the facts: four sealed boxes, not on the manifest, cold, humming, sealed with unknown codes. She sent it. Then she sat. The hum continued. The drone waited. The sky held its breath.  

Hours passed. No reply. The boxes remained. The hum deepened, a low, insistent sound that seemed to echo in the silence of the cabin. Ines stared at the readout, the four boxes glowing faintly, their seals unbroken. The drone’s systems were stable. The route was clear. But the cargo was not.  

She thought of the manifest. The medical stock. The spare parts. The route. The drone. The responsibility. She had signed for what was supposed to be there. But what was there was not on the list. And no one had told her.  

The hum grew louder. The boxes pulsed, as if breathing. Ines closed her eyes. The drone was her only witness. The sky was empty. The route was hers. And the cargo—four sealed boxes, cold and humming, not on the manifest—was the only thing that had changed.  

She waited. The hum never stopped. The boxes never opened. The drone flew on.
