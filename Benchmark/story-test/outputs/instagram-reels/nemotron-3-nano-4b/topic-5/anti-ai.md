What if a scribbled lemma could change everything?  
Retired topologist Helena Voss scribbled on an envelope.  
She called it the invariant.  
Her student Tomas Reyes called it beautiful.  
The argument ran three pages.  
It seemed elegant.  
It seemed robust.  
It rested on one lemma.  
Two boundary surfaces intersect.  
Helena wrote clearly.  
Nobody proved it.  
Tomas read it on a train.  
He tested small cases.  
The lemma held.  
But proof was different.  
They raced to close the gap.  
Helena wrote at night.  
Tomas replied with sketches.  
The conference was in six weeks.  
They knew the title slide.  
On final Friday.  
Tomas found a covering argument.  
It was not her proof.  
But it worked.  
They sat in her kitchen.  
They checked it twice.  
The lemma was no longer a gap.  
It was a step.
